The Anatomy of Revenue Durability™

Four pillars. One Half-Life. A number that moves.

Revenue Durability is the ability of your revenue to compound — not leak — over time. Measured not by what you sell, but by what survives, expands, and refers.

Measure your Half-Life → How we run engagements
Definition

Revenue durability is the ability of your revenue to compound — not leak — over time. NRR and GRR are the lagging indicators. Durability is the underlying property: do your customers buy for the right reasons, get to value fast, send clear signals when they drift or are ready to grow, and renew, expand and advocate through repeatable motions — all inside a CS function that runs without heroics?

The durability ledger

Every customer dollar you book sits in one of three buckets:

Durable revenue

Right-fit, well-onboarded, healthy usage, renewing and expanding. Compounds.

Latent leak

Looks fine on the dashboard but silently drifting. Will churn within two renewal cycles unless caught.

Dead-on-arrival

Wrong-fit at sale, never reached value. Charging them rent in your ARR line is a lie you're telling the board.

The job of the anatomy is to grow the first bucket and shrink the other two.

The four pillars

Sequential. You measure all four from day one, but you fix them in order.

Fit → Activation → Signal → Motions, standing on one foundation: the operating system. Fixing Signal or Motions before Fit is wallpaper over rot.

01

Pillar 1

Fit

Sell durable revenue. Filter out leaky revenue before it lands.

Durable revenue starts at the top of the funnel. The single most expensive thing a seed/A SaaS company does is sell to the wrong customer. The cost is not just the eventual churn — it's the engineering time, support load, and CSM capacity those customers consume on the way out the door.

Why it matters at seed/Series A

One wrong-fit logo at 8% of ARR can sink an entire NRR quarter. Sales–CS alignment on your Deep-Roots Customer is the single highest-leverage fix.

KPIs we watch

  • Deep-Roots fit rate of closed-won (target: 80%+)
  • Wrong-fit churn vs. Deep-Roots churn (wrong-fit no more than 2× Deep-Roots)
  • Time-to-disqualify in the sales process (< 2 calls for clear wrong-fit)
Operator take The cheapest churn save is the deal you didn't close.
02

Pillar 2

Activation

Get the customer to first value inside the first 30 days.

Onboarding is where most seed/A SaaS companies lose the most addressable revenue. It's treated as a project plan to be managed, when in fact it's the single most decisive moment in the customer relationship.

Why it matters at seed/Series A

Time-to-first-value is the single best predictor of 12-month retention. Founders almost always overestimate it.

KPIs we watch

  • Time-to-first-value in days (target: <30 SMB, <60 mid-market)
  • 30-day activation rate (target: 75%+)
  • 90-day activated vs. unactivated retention gap
Operator take The customer measures TTV from the day they signed the contract, not from the day you assigned a project manager.
03

Pillar 3

Signal

See drift before it leaves, and growth before it's asked for.

A signal is a change in what a customer does, who they are, or what they're getting that requires a defined response. Every signal points to risk, expansion or advocacy. Every signal has an owner and a play.

Signal decides what a CSM should notice. Motions decide what to do about it. Most seed/A companies have no health model, and the few that do treat it as a dashboard, not a workflow. A score that changes nothing is decoration.

Five kinds of signal

  • Relationship: a champion leaves, an exec sponsor goes quiet, a former champion lands at a new company.
  • Adoption: core-workflow usage drops, a feature gap appears or widens, the same question keeps coming back.
  • Value: the customer reaches, or misses, the outcome they bought for.
  • Commercial: procurement asks about terms, a seat or usage limit is hit, a new team asks for access.
  • Sentiment: ticket severity climbs, or the CSM has a bad feeling. Judgment counts when it's written down with a reason and a date.

What makes a signal strong

It shows up early enough to act on. A signal that fires late is history. After that, it has to be right often enough that the team keeps acting on it, because a signal that cries wolf gets ignored.

Who owns it

The CSM owns every signal. When the risk signals stack up, the CSM calls the Heal Desk.

Why it matters at seed/Series A

Without signals, the first time you learn a customer is unhappy is when they're drafting the cancel email. The first time you learn they're ready to grow is when they ask for something you never offered.

KPIs we watch

  • Churn surprise rate — % of churned or downsized accounts with no risk signal in the prior 90 days (target: <10%)
  • Signal lead time — median days from the first risk signal to the renewal date (target: 90+ days)
  • Signal-to-action time — from a signal firing to an owned action (target: <5 business days)
  • Play coverage — % of defined signals with a named play and owner (target: 100%)
Operator take The day a customer sends the cancel email is the day they decided three months ago. Your job is to be in the room three months ago.
04

Pillar 4

Motions

Four motions. Named plays, named owners, no heroics.

Signal sees it; Motions respond to it. A motion is a set of named plays, owned by named roles, triggered by named signals and producing named outcomes. There are four.

One rule runs through all of them: CS owns the relationship and the trigger. Others own the execution of non-CS plays: the AE writes the contract, the revenue leader approves the discount, product commits the roadmap.

Renew

The contract has an end date. The relationship shouldn't need one.

The renewal is decided in the first six to eight months of every contract year, through adoption and proven value, not at the renewal meeting. Renew has two halves. Earn it: the CSM runs adoption plays and proves value. Close it: the CSM and AE handle commercials and paper. On multi-year deals, every contract year gets the same treatment.

Save: the Heal Desk™

Sales has a deal desk. Customer Success should have a Heal Desk.

When it convenes: the CSM calls it when two or more risk signals fire, or one critical signal does, such as a champion leaving or procurement asking about termination terms. It meets within 3 business days.

Who sits at it: the CS leader, the revenue leader, product, the assigned CSM, and engineering when needed. The founder can sit in, but each leader's judgment carries their lane.

Who decides what: the CS leader owns the action plan and the voice of the customer, the revenue leader owns discounts, and product owns roadmap commitments. When lanes conflict, ARR and growth potential decide, and the CS leader makes the final call.

When it lets go: a customer who is wrong-fit by Pillar 1's criteria, or who costs more to serve than they pay, is let go, unless someone documents a strategic reason to keep them. Cost to serve is measured in CSM hours, support tickets and engineering hours.

Expand

The CSM owns the flag and the relationship; the AE owns the paper. Expansion signals become CSQLs (customer success qualified leads). RDG recommends compensating CSMs on CSQLs that convert.

Advocate

Advocacy is earned, triggered by a verified outcome rather than a calendar date. CS owns it end to end: the ask, the timing and the relationship. The ladder runs review → reference call → case study → referral → advisory board or speaking. Track who was asked and when, so the same three customers don't carry every ask.

Why it matters at seed/Series A

If renewals depend on a founder spreadsheet and a Wednesday-night call, and the only saves happen when the founder jumps on the phone, the function does not scale. Period.

KPIs we watch

  • Gross revenue retention (target: 90%+ mid-market, 85%+ SMB)
  • Net revenue retention (target: 110%+ mid-market, 100%+ SMB)
  • Renewal forecast accuracy (within 10% at 60 days out)
  • Expansion as % of new ARR (target: 30%+ by end of engagement)
  • Heal Desk save rate — % of Heal Desk accounts still customers at renewal (target: set at baseline)
  • CSQL conversion rate (target: set at baseline)
  • Referenceable customers as % of book (target: set at baseline)
Operator take "We'll figure out the renewal when we get there" is the most expensive sentence in seed/A SaaS. By the time you get there, the customer already has.
F

The foundation

Operating System

The CS function runs without you.

The four pillars are the work. The operating system is what makes the work happen without heroics: comp, tools, rhythms, hiring, documented playbooks and a seat at the leadership table. It isn't a fifth step you fix last. It's what every pillar stands on, and it's what an RDG engagement leaves behind.

Why it matters at seed/Series A

If you are personally the load-bearing wall in CS, the function is not yet a function. It's your second job.

How we measure it: Founder Load

Every pillar includes one founder-dependency question. Together they produce a Founder Load score alongside your Half-Life, so you can see whether the founder is the load-bearing wall, and where.

KPIs we watch

  • CS-as-% of revenue cost ratio (target: 8–12% at this stage)
  • Time-to-productivity of new CS hires (target: 60 days)
  • Founder time on CS execution (declining quarter over quarter to <5 hrs/week)
  • CS team retention (target: 90%+ annualized)
Operator take If the function only works when you're in the room, you don't have a function. You have a founder doing CS in a trench coat.
The Revenue Half-Life™

Twenty questions. Five per pillar. Scored 0–100.

Run in a 90-minute working session with the founder and the senior post-sale person, triangulated against a CRM and product-telemetry data pull. The Half-Life is the contract: baseline at kickoff, target for the engagement window, re-measure at the close. Every Half-Life comes with a Founder Load score.

ScoreBandWhat it means
0 – 40Leaky bucket Revenue is bleeding. Every quarter of delay raises the cost of the fix. Diagnostic + 90-day fix is the only sane move.
41 – 60Surviving Holding the line, mostly through founder effort. One bad quarter from a problem. Build the foundation before you scale GTM further.
61 – 80Durable The core is in place. Sharpen Signal and Motions and you compound. Series B becomes a defensible story.
